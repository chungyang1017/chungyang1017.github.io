⟦zh⟧ 當漫畫成真：日本的「夢想力」 ⟦en⟧ When the Manga Comes True: Japan's Power to Dream ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ When the Manga Comes True: Japan's Power to Dream — from Shohei Ohtani to the World Cup dream of Captain Tsubasa and Blue Lock ⟦en⟧ 當漫畫成真：日本的「夢想力」——從大谷翔平，到《足球小將》與《藍色監獄》的世界盃之夢 ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧葉崇揚 × Claude⟦en⟧Chung-Yang Yeh × Claude⟦/⟧ ⟦zh⟧2026 年 7 月 10 日⟦en⟧10 July 2026⟦/⟧ ⟦zh⟧日本 ・ Japan⟦en⟧Japan⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 棒球，才是日本真正的國球。滿田拓也的《棒球大聯盟》（MAJOR）裡，茂野吾郎從日本的少棒場，一路拚進美國大聯盟；在當年，「一個日本人稱霸美職」這種情節，聽起來就是只有漫畫才敢畫的白日夢。孩子闔上漫畫、走回現實，多半也明白那終究只是故事。 ⟦en⟧ Baseball, not football, is Japan's true national sport. In Takuya Mitsuda's manga MAJOR, Goro Shigeno claws his way from a Japanese little-league diamond all the way to the American major leagues; back then, the idea of a Japanese player conquering MLB sounded like the kind of daydream only a comic would dare to draw. A child would close the book, step back into the real world, and know, more or less, that it was only a story. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 然後，現實生出了一個大谷翔平——誇張到連漫畫都不敢這樣畫。他是投打二刀流，一個人同時是球隊的王牌投手，又是打線上最有威脅的強打者；二〇二四年，他打出大聯盟史上第一個「五十轟五十盜」，二〇二五年又敲出五十五支全壘打、還重新站回投手丘。他連續三年拿下全票最有價值球員（生涯第四座，史上只有貝瑞・邦茲比他多；而且這四座全是「全票」通過，前無古人）。這兩年，他更帶著道奇連莊世界大賽冠軍——同時做到衛冕冠軍又連莊 MVP 的，過去半個世紀只有一九七〇年代紅人隊的 Joe Morgan 辦到過。更別說二〇二三年世界棒球經典賽的冠軍戰，他在最後一個半局登板，親手三振掉自己在天使的當家隊友、超級球星楚奧特（Mike Trout），替日本封王；這種安排，連漫畫家都不一定敢寫。漫畫想像力的天花板，就這樣被現實一拳打穿。 ⟦en⟧ And then reality produced Shohei Ohtani — more outlandish than any manga would have dared. A two-way player, he is at once his team's ace pitcher and the most dangerous bat in its lineup. In 2024 he became the first man in major-league history to go 50-50 — fifty home runs and fifty stolen bases in a single season; in 2025 he hit fifty-five homers and climbed back onto the mound. He has taken the Most Valuable Player award unanimously for three straight years — his fourth in all, a tally surpassed only by Barry Bonds, and every one of his four won by a unanimous vote, a feat no player had managed before. Across these two seasons he has also carried the Dodgers to back-to-back World Series titles; the only other man in half a century to pair consecutive MVPs with consecutive championships was Joe Morgan of the 1970s Reds. And in the final of the 2023 World Baseball Classic, he took the mound for the last half-inning and struck out his own Angels teammate, the superstar Mike Trout, to win it for Japan — a script even a manga artist might have hesitated to write. The ceiling of the imagination, punched clean through by reality. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 這正是我想說的「夢想力」最迷人的地方：當一個社會持續敢做夢、又持續往夢裡蓋，它最後長出來的東西，會比它自己的虛構還要離譜。夢想力不是空想，是有工程圖的——敢做夢，而且願意規劃、願意動手。棒球是日本的國球，這份夢想力在這裡展現得最濃、也最理所當然。但真正讓我服氣的，是它連一項日本原本毫無天分的運動，都能硬生生改造過來——足球。而在足球這條路上，你可以把那台「造夢的引擎」看得一清二楚：漫畫。 ⟦en⟧ This is what I find most beguiling about what I would call Japan's "power to dream": when a society keeps daring to dream, and keeps building toward the dream, what it finally grows can be more improbable than its own fiction. The power to dream is not idle fantasy — it comes with a blueprint: dare to imagine, and then be willing to plan, and to do the work. Baseball is Japan's national game, and this power shows there most richly, most naturally. But what truly won me over is that the same force reshaped a sport at which Japan once had no gift at all — football. And on the football road, you can see the dream-engine with perfect clarity: manga. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 把時間拉回一九八一年。那一年，高橋陽一開始連載《足球小將》（キャプテン翼）。在那個日本足球還是一片荒地的年代——沒有職業聯賽、國際賽總是輸——這部漫畫先一步把足球畫得又熱血又快樂：大空翼那句「球是我的朋友」，帶球過人像跳舞，倒掛金鉤、必殺射門一記比一記誇張，還有若林源三、日向小次郎這些「既生瑜、何生亮」的對手與隊友。翼小將的夢很簡單也很大——披上日本隊球衣，把世界盃捧回來。這個夢，餵養了一整代日本小孩。那些看著翼小將長大的孩子，後來很多真的成了球員，成了把一九九八、二〇〇二撐起來的人；許多人也相信，日本基層足球的註冊人口會從八〇年代一路往上爬，這部漫畫正是點火的人之一。就連 Zidane、Messi、Iniesta、Torres 這些歐洲、南美的頂級球星，小時候也是看這部長大的。夢，是先被畫出來的，再被踢出來。 ⟦en⟧ Rewind to 1981. That year, Yoichi Takahashi began serialising Captain Tsubasa (キャプテン翼). In an age when Japanese football was still a wasteland — no professional league, defeat after defeat abroad — this comic drew the game as something joyful and thrilling before the country had ever lived it: Tsubasa Ozora's line, "the ball is my friend"; dribbles like dancing; bicycle kicks and impossible shots, each wilder than the last; rivals and comrades like Genzo Wakabayashi and Kojiro Hyuga. Tsubasa's dream was simple and enormous — pull on the Japan shirt and bring home the World Cup. That dream fed a whole generation of Japanese children. Many who grew up on Tsubasa really did become players — the ones who carried 1998 and 2002; and many believe the steady climb in Japan's grassroots registration from the 1980s onward was, in no small part, a fire this manga lit. Even world-class stars in Europe and South America — Zidane, Messi, Iniesta, Torres — grew up reading it. The dream was drawn first, and only then kicked into being. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 畫歸畫，日本也真的動手去蓋。日本足協在二〇〇五年立下一個聽起來近乎狂妄的目標：二〇五〇年以前，要在自己的土地上辦一屆世界盃，並且由日本隊捧起冠軍；那份宣言還配了一句副標——「夢があるから強くなる」，因為有夢，才會變強。而且他們把這個大到嚇人的夢，切成一格一格的階段往前推。這種蓋法，其實一九九三年就開始了：那年五月，日本職業足球聯賽（J聯盟）正式開踢，用最笨也最實在的方法，從把土壤養好做起。可是同一年十月，日本在多哈對伊拉克的世界盃資格賽最後一戰，眼看只要守住比分就能第一次晉級，卻在終場前的最後一擊被追平，夢在幾秒之內碎掉——這就是日本球迷永遠記得的「多哈悲劇」。換作別的地方，這種痛也許就讓人算了；日本沒有。聯賽照辦，青訓照蓋，四年後的一九九七年，靠岡野雅行在延長賽的黃金進球擊敗伊朗，第一次拿到世界盃門票，那一夜被稱作「柔佛巴魯的狂喜」。一九九八年法國世界盃，日本終於站上了那個他們追了幾十年的舞台。而這一整段歷史，漫畫也同步畫著：村枝賢一的《足球好小子》（俺たちのフィールド）從一九九二畫到一九九八，幾乎踩著真實事件在走——J聯盟開幕畫了，多哈悲劇畫了，最後一回，就停在一九九八年日本對阿根廷的世界盃首戰。現實在球場上發生什麼，漫畫就在同一時間，把它畫成故事。 ⟦en⟧ Drawn is one thing; Japan also went and built it. In 2005 the Japan Football Association set a goal that sounded almost delusional: to host a World Cup on home soil before 2050, and to have Japan lift the trophy. The declaration even carried a tagline — yume ga aru kara tsuyoku naru, because we have a dream, we grow strong — and they cut that terrifyingly large dream into stages, and pushed forward one frame at a time. That way of building had in fact begun in 1993: that May, the J.League kicked off, in the plainest, most patient way imaginable, by first tending the soil. Yet that very October, in the final World Cup qualifier against Iraq in Doha, with Japan needing only to hold the lead to qualify for the first time, an equaliser in the dying seconds shattered the dream in an instant — the "Agony of Doha" that Japanese fans will never forget. Elsewhere, that kind of pain might have made a nation give up. Japan did not. The league played on, the academies kept building, and four years later, in 1997, a golden goal from Masayuki Okano in extra time beat Iran and won a World Cup ticket for the first time — a night remembered as the "Joy of Johor Bahru." At France 1998, Japan finally stood on the stage it had chased for decades. And all of it was being drawn in real time: Kenichi Muraeda's Our Field (俺たちのフィールド) ran from 1992 to 1998, tracking real events almost step for step — the J.League's opening, the Agony of Doha — and its final chapter stopped at Japan's opening match against Argentina at France 1998. Whatever happened on the pitch, the manga turned it into a story at the very same moment. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 其實，日本能走到今天，靠的不只是敢贏，更是懂得怎麼面對輸。二〇一八年俄羅斯世界盃十六強，日本對比利時一度二比零領先——那大概是他們史上最接近八強的一次，近到幾乎能碰到那扇門的把手。可是比利時把比分追了回來，最後在第九十四分鐘，用一次教科書般的閃電反擊，從日本的一記角球一路推到對面球門，補時絕殺，三比二。終場哨響，日本球員一個個癱倒在草皮上，全世界都替他們心碎。但真正了不起的，是那之後：日本沒有把它當成一次倒楣就翻頁，而是把那短短十幾秒拆開來、一格一格地看——球是怎麼被反擊的？那幾秒鐘，我們到底少做了什麼？此後好幾年，這場被稱為「羅斯托夫的十四秒」的敗仗，成了日本足球反覆咀嚼的教材。因為對他們來說，失敗從來不可怕；真正可怕的，是失敗得毫無意義、什麼都沒學到，就這樣算了。 ⟦en⟧ In truth, Japan got here not only by daring to win, but by learning how to lose. At the 2018 World Cup in Russia, in the round of 16, Japan led Belgium 2-0 — probably the closest they had ever come to the quarter-finals, close enough to touch the handle of the door. But Belgium clawed it back, and in the 94th minute, with a textbook lightning counterattack that ran the length of the pitch from a Japanese corner, sank them at the death, 3-2. When the whistle went, the Japanese players dropped to the turf, and the whole world's heart broke for them. But the remarkable thing came afterward: Japan did not file it away as bad luck and turn the page. They took those dozen-odd seconds apart, frame by frame — how did the counter unfold? In those seconds, what did we fail to do? For years, that defeat, known as "the 14 Seconds of Rostov," became a text Japanese football chewed over again and again. Because to them, failure was never the thing to fear. What was truly to be feared was a failure that meant nothing — that taught you nothing, and was simply let go. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 那股「把失敗磨成養分」的狠勁，四年後就在卡達開了花。二〇二二年世界盃，日本被分進所謂的「死亡之組」，同組站著兩支捧過世界盃的老牌霸主——德國和西班牙。對德國那場，日本上半場先失一球，換作以前大概就低頭了；但這一次，他們下半場換上生力軍，靠堂安律和淺野拓磨連扳兩城，二比一逆轉，把四星德國逼得當場失措。對西班牙，劇本幾乎重演：又是先落後，又是堂安律扳平，接著是那一顆全日本至今說起來還會屏住呼吸的球——三笘薫追著一顆看起來早已滾出底線的死球，就是不放棄，在最後一刻把它撈了回來、倒三角回傳，田中碧推進空門。慢動作重播裡，那顆球只剩薄薄一毫米還壓在線上，日本人後來就叫它「三笘的一毫米」。就是這一毫米、這一次「球還沒出界以前，我絕不鬆腳」的倔強，把日本頂上了小組第一，也把昔日的世界冠軍德國，硬生生擋在了十六強門外。那一刻，多少人脫口而出：這根本就是《足球小將》的劇本成真了。 ⟦en⟧ That grit — grinding failure into fuel — bore fruit four years later in Qatar. At the 2022 World Cup, Japan were drawn into a so-called "group of death," standing alongside two former world champions: Germany and Spain. Against Germany, Japan fell behind in the first half; once, that would have been the end of it. This time, they sent on fresh legs after the break, and goals from Ritsu Doan and Takuma Asano turned it around, 2-1, leaving the four-star Germans stunned. Against Spain, the script all but repeated: behind again, level again through Doan — and then the ball the whole of Japan still holds its breath to describe. Kaoru Mitoma chased down a ball that looked, for all the world, to have already rolled over the byline, refused to give up, hooked it back at the last instant and cut it across for Ao Tanaka to bundle home. In the slow-motion replay, a mere millimetre of the ball was still touching the line; Japan came to call it "Mitoma's Millimetre." It was that millimetre — that single refusal to lift a foot before the ball was truly out — that put Japan top of the group, and shut the former world champions of Germany out of the last sixteen altogether. And in that moment, how many blurted it out: this is Captain Tsubasa come true. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 而日本的夢，也就在這樣一次次「差一點」與「終於做到了」之間，悄悄升級了——從「能不能踢進世界盃」，變成「憑什麼還贏不了」。而這股新的野心，漫畫又一次同步畫了出來：金城宗幸原作、ノ村優介作畫的《藍色監獄》（ブルーロック，二〇一八年起連載）。它的前提，幾乎是把日本足協的焦慮直接搬上紙面——日本一再止步十六強，到底缺什麼？答案是：缺一個能在世界舞台上一劍封喉的前鋒。於是故事虛構出一座叫「藍色監獄」的設施，把全國幾百個最有天分的前鋒關進去，用近乎殘酷、極致利己的競爭，去淬煉出那唯一一個能替日本奪下世界盃的射手。主角潔世一要的，不再只是「參與」，而是「成為世界第一」。從大空翼的「球是我的朋友」，到潔世一的「我要成為世界最強前鋒」，你會發現夢的等級整個換了一階——從愛上足球，到非贏不可。 ⟦en⟧ And so, between these near-misses and finally-did-its, Japan's dream quietly leveled up — from "can we even reach the World Cup" to "why aren't we winning it yet." That new ambition, too, the manga drew in step: Blue Lock (ブルーロック, serialised from 2018), written by Muneyuki Kaneshiro and drawn by Yusuke Nomura. Its premise is almost the JFA's anxiety set straight onto the page — Japan keeps stalling in the round of 16, so what is missing? The answer: a striker who can deliver the killing blow on the world stage. So the story invents a facility called "Blue Lock," locks up several hundred of the nation's most gifted strikers, and through a nearly cruel, radically selfish competition forges the one striker who can win Japan the World Cup. Its hero, Yoichi Isagi, no longer wants merely to "take part"; he wants to become the best in the world. From Tsubasa's "the ball is my friend" to Isagi's "I will become the world's greatest striker," you can feel the dream shift up an entire gear — from loving the game, to having to win it. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 而漫畫餵給孩子的，從來不只是奪冠的夢，還有跌倒之後怎麼再站起來。田中モトユキ的《BE BLUES！～青になれ～》，主角一条龍原是眾人看好的天才少年，卻在入選少年國家隊的那一天被車撞成重傷，一度連還能不能再踢球都成了問號；整部漫畫，講的就是他怎麼從那個谷底、一寸一寸地把自己爬回綠茵場。這種「跌到最底、再重新站起來」的故事，和日本足球在多哈、在羅斯托夫親身學到的，其實是同一件事——漫畫不只教孩子做夢，也教他們在夢碎之後，怎麼把自己一塊一塊拼回去。再加上《足球風雲》（シュート！）這些一部接一部的作品，足球漫畫就像一條不斷電的輸送帶，一代又一代，把「敢做夢、也敢跌倒重來」這件事，交到孩子手上。 ⟦en⟧ And what the manga feeds children was never only the dream of winning, but how to rise after you fall. In Motoyuki Tanaka's BE BLUES! ~Ao ni Nare~, the hero Ryu Ichijo — once a prodigy everyone tipped for greatness — is struck by a car on the very day he is called up to the youth national team, and badly hurt; for a while, whether he can even play again is an open question, and the whole manga is about how he claws his way, inch by inch, back onto the pitch. That story of falling to the very bottom and standing up again is, in the end, the same thing Japanese football learned first-hand in Doha and in Rostov — the manga teaches children not only to dream, but how, once a dream shatters, to put themselves back together piece by piece. Add Shoot! (シュート！) and the rest, one after another, and football manga becomes a conveyor belt that never powers down, handing "dare to dream, and dare to get up again" from one generation to the next. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 而就在寫下這篇文章的此刻，二〇二六年的世界盃正在美加墨三國的球場上打著，你幾乎能看見那條從一九八一年一路鋪過來的路。小組賽首戰，面對那支曾讓整個亞洲仰望的橙衣荷蘭，日本在落後之下始終不肯認輸、一路苦苦追趕，直到終場前，才由鎌田大地把比分硬生生扳成二比二。想想看：三十幾年前還是一片荒地、國際賽逢戰必輸的日本足球，如今能站在荷蘭面前扳平離場、眼神裡沒有半分膽怯——光是這一幕，本身就是一種宣告。接著一場四比零血洗突尼西亞，成為世界盃史上第一支單場踢進四球的亞洲球隊。開賽才第四分鐘，鎌田就攻進日本世界盃史上最快的一顆進球；而全場最讓人起雞皮疙瘩的，是那記行雲流水的第三球——田中碧從自家陣地送出一記銳利的直塞，上田綺世不停球、用右腳輕輕一撥，一記絕妙的觸傳就把球送穿突尼西亞的最後防線，早已插到身後的伊東純也單刀面對門將、冷靜推射入網。從後場直塞、到前場一觸即發的挑傳、再到伊東從防線背後殺出的破門，兩三下就一氣呵成，順得像《足球小將》裡才畫得出來的分鏡（別忘了，那場三笘薫和久保建英兩張王牌都因傷缺陣，日本照樣贏得從容）。最後一戰再和瑞典一比一言和，日本以小組第二晉級，然後在三十二強撞上五度奪冠的巴西。他們一路拚到終場，仍被馬丁內利（Gabriel Martinelli）在尾聲一劍封喉，一比二落敗。哨音響起，日本又一次倒在淘汰賽的門口——是的，直到今天，日本從來沒有贏過任何一場世界盃的淘汰賽。 ⟦en⟧ And at the very moment I write this, the 2026 World Cup is being played out across stadiums in the United States, Canada, and Mexico — and you can almost see the road that has been laid, frame by frame, since 1981. In their opening match, against the orange of the Netherlands — a side all of Asia once looked up to — Japan, trailing, simply refused to concede the point, chasing and chasing until, in the closing minutes, Daichi Kamada dragged the score level at 2-2. Think about it: a Japanese football that thirty-odd years ago was a wasteland, losing every game it played, can now stand before the Netherlands, draw level and walk off without a flicker of fear in its eyes — that alone is a kind of declaration. Then came a 4-0 rout of Tunisia, making Japan the first Asian side ever to score four in a single World Cup match. Barely four minutes in, Kamada scored the fastest goal in Japan's World Cup history; but the moment that raised every hair was the flowing third goal — Ao Tanaka drove a sharp vertical pass out of the back, Ayase Ueda flicked it first-time with his right foot, an exquisite touch that threaded it through Tunisia's last line, and Junya Ito, already breaking in behind, calmly slid it past the keeper one-on-one. From the ball out of defence, to the split-second flick up front, to Ito bursting through the back line — two or three touches, seamless, as smooth as a panel out of Captain Tsubasa (and remember: both of Japan's aces, Kaoru Mitoma and Takefusa Kubo, were sidelined by injury that night, and Japan still won at their ease). A 1-1 draw with Sweden in the final group game sent Japan through as runners-up, and then, in the round of 32, they ran into Brazil, five-time world champions. They fought to the final whistle, only for Gabriel Martinelli to settle it late, 2-1. When the whistle blew, Japan had fallen at the knockout door once more — for yes, to this day, Japan has never won a single World Cup knockout match. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 所以日本厲害的地方，不只是敢喊一個大到別人不敢喊的目標。他們喊完之後，會認真去蓋實現它的那套系統。文化上，有漫畫這台造夢的引擎，從《足球小將》到《藍色監獄》，一棒接一棒；制度上，有 J聯盟和足協這台落地的引擎，青訓學院、一格一格往前推的十年、二十年計畫。兩台引擎朝同一個方向使力：漫畫讓孩子敢想，聯賽和足協讓敢想的孩子有地方去，這兩件事一接上，夢就開始自己往前滾。而且他們夠有耐性，知道地基得花十年、二十年才蓋得起來，就真的一年一年蓋下去，不會因為一次多哈、一次巴西的失敗就掀桌走人。 ⟦en⟧ So what makes Japan formidable is not merely that it dares to name a goal too large for others to say aloud. Having said it, they go and earnestly build the system to make it real. Culturally, there is the dream-engine of manga, from Captain Tsubasa to Blue Lock, one baton after another; institutionally, there is the grounding-engine of the J.League and the JFA — the academies, the ten- and twenty-year plans pushed forward one frame at a time. The two engines pull in the same direction: manga lets children dare to imagine, the league and the association give those daring children somewhere to go, and once the two connect, the dream begins to roll forward on its own. And they are patient enough — knowing the foundation takes ten or twenty years to lay — to keep laying it, year after year, without flipping the table over a single Doha, a single Brazil. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 東亞其他國家，坦白說不太做得到這件事。有的很敢喊願景，卻沒耐性把地基蓋完；有的很會埋頭苦幹，卻不太敢把夢做得那麼大。日本難得的，是把「敢做夢」和「肯規劃、肯行動」這兩件常常互相拉扯的事，湊到了一起。 ⟦en⟧ Other countries in East Asia, frankly, cannot quite pull this off. Some are bold enough to proclaim a vision but lack the patience to finish the foundation; others put their heads down and toil but don't quite dare to dream that big. What is rare about Japan is that it has brought together two things that so often pull against each other — daring to dream, and being willing to plan and to act. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 而最讓我動容的，其實是這件事的另一面。從一九九三年多哈那個在幾秒之內碎掉的夢，到二〇二六年在巴西面前差一步的夢，中間隔了三十三年——三十三年，一次又一次走到淘汰賽的門口，一次又一次被擋在門外。換作別人，早該認命說一句「我們大概就到這裡了」。可是日本沒有。他們不怕輸，最怕的是輸得沒有意義；於是多哈也好、羅斯托夫也好、這一次的巴西也好，每一次心碎，都被他們彎下腰撿起來、拆開來看清楚，再變成下一次的養分。輸完的隔天，聯賽照踢，青訓照蓋，孩子照樣捧著漫畫，把自己想像成下一個大空翼、下一個潔世一。而那些當年真的看著翼小將長大的孩子，如今散落在整個歐洲的草皮上——光是二〇二五／二六賽季，就有一百一十幾位日本球員在歐洲各級聯賽踢球，短短五年之間成長了九成。夢沒有留在紙上，它長成了一整個世代，一個一個，把自己踢進了世界最高的舞台。敢做夢真正動人的地方，從來不是它一次就成功；而是它明明一次次落空，卻總還有人願意再相信一次、再蓋一年、再把球傳給下一個孩子。 ⟦en⟧ And what moves me most is, in fact, the other side of all this. From the dream that shattered in seconds at Doha in 1993, to the dream that fell a step short against Brazil in 2026, thirty-three years have passed — thirty-three years of walking up to the knockout door, again and again, and being shut out, again and again. Anyone else would long since have resigned themselves: "this is probably as far as we go." Japan did not. They do not fear losing; what they fear is losing for nothing — and so Doha, and Rostov, and this time Brazil, every heartbreak, they bend down and pick up, take apart and look at clearly, and turn into fuel for the next time. The day after a loss, the league plays on, the academies keep building, and children still cradle their manga, imagining themselves the next Tsubasa, the next Isagi. And those children who really did grow up on Tsubasa are now scattered across the pitches of Europe — in the 2025-26 season alone, more than a hundred Japanese players were turning out across the divisions of Europe, a ninety-per-cent rise in just five years. The dream did not stay on the page; it grew into an entire generation, kicking their way, one by one, onto the highest stage in the world. What is truly moving about daring to dream is never that it succeeds on the first try; it is that, even as it falls short time after time, there is always someone willing to believe once more, to build one more year, to pass the ball to the next child. ⟦/⟧

⟦zh⟧ 回到那句「因為有夢，才會變強」——它的順序始終是夢在前、強在後：先允許自己去想一件看起來太遠、甚至有點好笑的事，再一格一格、一年一年，把路鋪過去。從大空翼的「球是我的朋友」，到潔世一的「我要成為世界第一」，再到今天一百多個把自己踢進歐洲的日本孩子，我們親眼看著一個社會，怎麼把漫畫裡的分鏡，一格一格、一棒一腳，打成、也踢成現實。而如果要我指出這股「夢想力」最好的例子，那一定是大谷翔平——他就是這股力量最完整、也最驚人的結晶：一個把「連漫畫都不敢畫」活成日常的人，一個親手證明了「先敢做夢，再一格一格把它蓋出來」到底能走多遠的男人。一個能把大空翼養成大谷翔平的社會，要把《足球小將》與《藍色監獄》裡那座世界盃也踢成真，剩下的，也許真的只是——哪一屆而已。所以現在，當日本人說他們要在二〇五〇年以前捧起世界盃，我已經一點都笑不出來了。我甚至，有點相信。 ⟦en⟧ Return to that line — "because we have a dream, we grow strong." The order is always dream first, strength after: first allow yourself to imagine something that looks too far off, even a little laughable, and then, frame by frame, year by year, lay the road toward it. From Tsubasa's "the ball is my friend," to Isagi's "I will become the best in the world," to the hundred-plus Japanese children who have kicked their way into Europe today, we have watched a society turn the panels of a comic — frame by frame, pitch by pitch — into reality. And if you asked me to name the finest example of this power to dream, it could only be Shohei Ohtani: the most complete, most astonishing crystallisation of that force — a man who lives "what even a manga wouldn't dare draw" as his ordinary day, who has shown with his own hands how far "dare to dream first, then build it frame by frame" can go. A society that can raise a Tsubasa into an Ohtani — for it to turn the World Cup of Captain Tsubasa and Blue Lock real as well, the only question left may truly be: which tournament. So now, when the Japanese say they mean to lift the World Cup before 2050, I cannot bring myself to laugh anymore. I even, a little, believe them. ⟦/⟧
