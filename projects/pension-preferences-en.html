The Multidimensional Pension Preferences:
Information, the Politics of Trade-offs, and Pension Preferences in East Asia

Pension reform is a challenge East Asian welfare states share: institutions must respond to population ageing and fiscal pressure, yet reform stalls in the face of public opinion. This project asks — where do public preferences for pension reform come from, how are they moved by information, and how are they won over by the politics of trade-offs?

Three Axes研究主軸

Multidimensional preferences

Preferences for pension reform are not a simple yes or no. Unpacking them into trade-offs over benefit levels, financing mixes, retirement ages and intergenerational allocation maps the inner structure of public preferences.

Information

Public understanding of pension systems is often limited. When information about system finances, demographic trends and reform consequences is laid out, how do preferences move? This axis examines the role of information in preference formation.

The politics of trade-offs

Reform is never a referendum on a single policy; it is an exchange of packages and compensation. Which combinations of trade-offs make painful reform acceptable to the public?

Research Design研究設計

The project is built around cross-national survey experiments fielded simultaneously across East Asian societies — conjoint experiments ask respondents to choose between complete reform packages rather than react to single policies.
