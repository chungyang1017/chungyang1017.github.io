About

Writing between institutions and society — a researcher of the welfare state.

A welfare-state researcher. After completing my PhD in the United Kingdom, I brought a comparative welfare-regime lens back to Taiwan and East Asia. My long-standing concerns are pensions, social investment, welfare attitudes, and inequality.

My work moves between comparative and institutional analysis. I want the prose to be hard and the vision soft — data should be verifiable, yet society always holds one more layer than the model.

Recent interests run to welfare markets, the financialisation of welfare, and the rise of the regulatory welfare state. I am also asking how social policy should answer to a Taiwan that keeps shrinking, ageing, and growing more unequal.

Photography

Also a photographer. For nearly thirty years, photography has been another way of seeing that runs alongside the research. I photograph both landscapes and streets, in digital and on film.

In landscape, I look for a photograph with feeling and personal emotion rather than a pretty view: to express what I saw through my own emotion. I am fond of the blue hour, because what is photographed at that time is different. I also love the Impressionists and Turner, who express emotion through the composition of colours while reflecting the light.

Street photography carries more social responsibility. It is not only an outlet for personal emotion; it is also the photographer’s critique of existing social structures.

My taste leans towards Japanese photography. The photographers I admire include Shoji Ueda, Shinzo Maeda and Toshiki Nakanishi, as well as Josef Koudelka and André Kertész.
